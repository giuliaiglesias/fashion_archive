What happened

Gucci was struggling in the mid-1990s, and Tom Ford had almost quit after the previous season did badly. For this show he sent out more than 50 looks: low-waisted, hip-hugging velvet trousers, jewel-colored satin blouses, 1970s-style suits, shaggy hair and dark eyeliner.

Ford had the backlight switched off, so that the audience watched the models and the clothes and not the famous guests in the front row. At the end he took a bow, which, according to one account, went against his contract.

Why it mattered

The show is remembered as the moment Gucci became cool again. The New York Times critic Amy Spindler credited Ford with bringing cool back to luxury fashion. The low-waisted trouser spread in 1996, when designers such as Calvin Klein, Versace and Chanel showed their own versions. Madonna wore one of the satin shirts to the 1995 MTV Video Music Awards, and Gucci went public the same year.
