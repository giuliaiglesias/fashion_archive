What happened

The clothes were stiff and not at all flattering on purpose: skirts as long as a librarian’s, prints that looked like formica tabletops, a trompe-l’oeil tweed print and nylon used for more than bags. The greens were so strange that one critic, Robin Givhan, described them as sitting somewhere between “slime and mold”. The shoes were chunky T-bar sandals and sliders with oversized buttons, flat or low-heeled, in a decade that loved high heels.

Kristen McMenamy opened the show, followed by Amber Valletta, Kate Moss, Shalom Harlow, Kirsty Hume and Carolyn Murphy. Murphy, with a pixie haircut, was also the face of the season’s ad campaign.

Why it mattered

People either hated it or loved it. Givhan’s review in the Washington Post in May 1996 was called “Ugly is in”, and Tim Blanks called it Prada’s “pretty/ugly” turning point. It is credited with starting “ugly chic”, and the writer Alexander Fury compared its influence to Dior’s New Look of 1947. It also made chocolate brown popular and sent people looking for vintage clothes.
