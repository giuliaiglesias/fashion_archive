The 1990s

1990

  • Designer

    Tom Ford: Moves to Milan and joins Gucci as a women’s ready-to-wear designer.

  • Designer

    Jean Paul Gaultier: Creates the cone-shaped bra for Madonna’s Blond Ambition tour and publishes his autobiography.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell: Appears in George Michael’s video for “Freedom! ’90”.

  • Model

    Kate Moss: Photographs by Corinne Day in The Face make her the “anti-supermodel”.

  • Model

    Linda Evangelista (January 1990): British Vogue cover with four other top models. Becomes the face of Revlon.

  • Model

    Christy Turlington (January 1990): British Vogue cover that inspired the “Freedom! ’90” video.

1991

  • Runway

    Versace: The Supermodel Finale — Autumn/Winter 1991 · Milan.

  • Designer

    Valentino Garavani: Sophia Loren receives her honorary Oscar in a black sequinned Valentino dress.

  • Designer

    Gianni Versace: The Autumn/Winter show ends with four supermodels lip-synching to “Freedom! ’90”. Some accounts also name Claudia Schiffer.

  • Designer

    John Galliano: Makes his catwalk debut in Paris.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell (September 1991): Cover of Time. The same year she is one of four models in the Versace finale.

  • Model

    Linda Evangelista (Autumn/Winter 1991): One of four models in the Versace finale.

  • Model

    Christy Turlington (Autumn/Winter 1991): One of four models in the Versace finale.

1992

  • Runway

    Perry Ellis: The Grunge Collection — Spring/Summer 1993.

  • Designer

    Valentino Garavani: His couture collection includes the black-and-white dress Julia Roberts will wear to win her Oscar in 2001.

  • Designer

    Marc Jacobs: Wins the CFDA award for Womenswear Designer of the Year.

  • Designer

    Marc Jacobs: Shows the Spring/Summer 1993 grunge collection. Perry Ellis fires him a few weeks later and the clothes are never produced.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell: Appears in Madonna’s book Sex and her “Erotica” video.

  • Model

    Kate Moss: Signs with Calvin Klein.

  • Model

    Christy Turlington: Becomes the face of Maybelline. Opens the Perry Ellis grunge show.

1993

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada: Launches Miu Miu and the Fondazione Prada.

  • Designer

    Marc Jacobs: Relaunches his own label as Marc Jacobs International.

  • Designer

    Jean Paul Gaultier: Launches perfumes sold in corset-shaped bottles.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell (Spring/Summer 1993): Walks in Marc Jacobs’ grunge show for Perry Ellis.

  • Model

    Kate Moss (Spring/Summer 1993): Closes Marc Jacobs’ grunge show for Perry Ellis.

  • Model

    Amber Valletta (February 1993): First cover of American Vogue.

  • Model

    Christy Turlington: Named the “Face of the 20th Century” by the Metropolitan Museum of Art.

1994

  • Designer

    Gianni Versace: Elizabeth Hurley wears his safety-pin dress to the premiere of Four Weddings and a Funeral.

  • Designer

    Tom Ford: Becomes Gucci’s creative director at 33.

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada (Fall 1994): Uses the nylon from her backpacks for jackets, vests, dresses and skirts.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell (Fall 1994): Walks Prada’s show in a nylon dress.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen: Spotted at 14 in a shopping mall by an agent from Elite Model Management.

1995

  • Runway

    Gucci: Velvet and Satin — Fall/Winter 1995 · Milan.

  • Designer

    Alexander McQueen: Introduces the “bumster” trousers and shows “Highland Rape”. His spring collection is named after Hitchcock’s The Birds.

  • Designer

    Tom Ford: His Fall collection, with velvet hipster suits, gets a huge reaction, and Gucci goes public the same year.

  • Designer

    Donatella Versace: Gianni launches the fragrance Blonde in her honor.

  • Model

    Kate Moss (Fall/Winter 1995): Walks Tom Ford’s Gucci show.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen: Second in Brazil and fourth in the world in Elite’s Look of the Year contest.

  • Model

    Amber Valletta (Fall/Winter 1995): Walks Tom Ford’s Gucci show.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow: First winner of the Vogue/VH1 Model of the Year award.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow (Fall/Winter 1995): Walks Tom Ford’s Gucci show.

1996

  • Runway

    Prada: Banal Eccentricity — Spring/Summer 1996.

  • Designer

    Alexander McQueen (October 1996): LVMH names him head of Givenchy, replacing John Galliano. He is also named British Designer of the Year.

  • Designer

    John Galliano (January 1996): Presents his first show for Givenchy, the first British designer at a French fashion house since World War II. Dior hires him within a year.

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada: Collections described as “geek chic” and “jolie laide”, French for “pretty ugly”.

  • Model

    Kate Moss (Spring/Summer 1996): Walks Prada’s “Banal Eccentricity” show.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen: Moves to New York to start her career.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: Second place at Ford’s Supermodel of the World contest.

  • Model

    Amber Valletta (Spring/Summer 1996): Walks Prada’s “Banal Eccentricity” show.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow (Spring/Summer 1996): Walks Prada’s “Banal Eccentricity” show.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow (1996 to 1997): Co-hosts MTV’s House of Style.

1997

  • Runway

    Gaultier: Atmosphere of a Couture Salon — Spring/Summer 1997 · Paris.

  • Designer

    Alexander McQueen (January 1997): Presents his first haute couture collection for Givenchy.

  • Designer

    Gianni Versace: On 15 July he is shot outside his home in Miami Beach. Donatella becomes chief designer, and his brother Santo continues to run the business side.

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Spring/Summer 1997): His first haute couture collection for Dior.

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada: Introduces Prada Sport, luxury clothes for exercise or “roughing it”.

  • Designer

    Marc Jacobs: Becomes creative director of Louis Vuitton, until 2013.

  • Designer

    Donatella Versace: After Gianni’s death she becomes chief designer and vice president of the board, with a 20 percent stake.

  • Designer

    Jean Paul Gaultier: Launches his first haute couture line.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen (October 1997): Cover of Brazilian Vogue.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: Moves to New York. Runway debut at Anna Sui’s show.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow: Appears in the film In & Out.

  • Model

    Linda Evangelista: Lifetime Achievement Award from Vogue and VH1.

1998

  • Runway

    McQueen: No. 13 — Spring/Summer 1999 · London.

  • Runway

    Dior: A Cornucopia of Excess — Spring/Summer 1998 · Paris.

  • Designer

    Valentino Garavani: He and Giammetti sell the company. Valentino stays on as its designer.

  • Designer

    Alexander McQueen: Shows a collection in which a model stands in a ring of fire.

  • Designer

    Alexander McQueen (September 1998): “No. 13” (the Spring/Summer 1999 collection), held in the Gatliff Warehouse, a former bus depot in Victoria, London. Paralympian Aimee Mullins opens it in carved wooden legs designed by McQueen, and robots spray Shalom Harlow’s white dress black and yellow. McQueen said he was inspired by Rebecca Horn’s 1991 artwork High Moon.

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Spring/Summer 1998): A couture show inspired by the heiress Marchesa Casati, with her portrait by Giovanni Boldini on the invitations.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell: Produces a fashion show in South Africa for the Nelson Mandela Children’s Fund.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima (September 1998): First magazine cover, Marie Claire Brazil.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow (September 1998): Finale of Alexander McQueen’s “No. 13” (Spring/Summer 1999).

1999

  • Runway

    Versace: The Jungle Dress — Spring/Summer 2000 · Milan.

  • Designer

    Tom Ford: Gucci Group buys Yves Saint Laurent, and Ford also becomes its creative director.

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada (Spring 1999): Makes fanny packs a must-have accessory with “tourista chic”.

  • Designer

    Donatella Versace (Autumn 1999): The green jungle-print dress appears on the Spring/Summer 2000 runway in Milan, worn by Amber Valletta.

  • Designer

    Donatella Versace (6 December 1999): Ginger Spice and Donatella herself wear it at the Met Gala.

  • Designer

    Jean Paul Gaultier: Hermès buys a 35 percent stake in his company.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell: Launches a line of women’s perfumes.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen (July 1999): Cover of American Vogue, “Return of the Sexy Model”. Named Model of the Year by Vogue and VH1.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: First Victoria’s Secret runway show, at 17.

  • Model

    Amber Valletta (November 1999): Vogue Millennium cover, “Modern Muses”.

  • Model

    Christy Turlington: Graduates with honors from New York University.

The 2000s

2000

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Spring/Summer 2000): A show inspired by people without homes that he saw on his daily runs in Paris: threadbare clothes, empty bottles and dangling rubbish.

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Autumn/Winter 2000): A show imagined as a society wedding, opening with a bishop-shaped gown.

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada: The Prada bowling bag becomes “the It bag” of the year, and lip-print skirts appear.

  • Designer

    Donatella Versace: Jennifer Lopez wears it at the Grammy Awards, and the Palazzo Versace hotel opens in Australia.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen: Signs with Victoria’s Secret, until 2007.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: Becomes a Victoria’s Secret Angel and a Guess girl. First Vogue covers in September.

  • Model

    Amber Valletta (Spring/Summer 2000): Wears the Versace jungle dress on the runway in Milan.

  • Model

    Amber Valletta: Her first film, Drop Back Ten, and What Lies Beneath are released.

2001

  • Designer

    Valentino Garavani: Julia Roberts wins Best Actress in that dress, and Jennifer Lopez marries in a white lace Valentino gown.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow: Appears in Vanilla Sky.

2003

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Spring/Summer 2003): An “Asian” collection with circus performers after a trip to China and Japan. Dior’s couture division reports a 41 percent rise in yearly revenue.

  • Designer

    Jean Paul Gaultier: Becomes head designer at Hermès, a job he keeps until 2011.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: Opens the Victoria’s Secret show. Starts as Maybelline spokesmodel, until 2009.

  • Model

    Doutzen Kroes: Starts modeling after sending snapshots to an Amsterdam agency. Cast by Victoria’s Secret in New York.

  • Model

    Linda Evangelista: Gets a star on Canada’s Walk of Fame in Toronto.

2004

  • Designer

    Valentino Garavani: Jennifer Aniston wears the red “Fiesta” dress to a London film premiere.

  • Designer

    Tom Ford (April 2004): Leaves Gucci Group after disagreements about creative control.

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Autumn/Winter 2004): The model Erin O’Connor opens as Nefertiti in silver lamé and gold snakeskin. Galliano calls the shape the “sphinx line”.

2005

  • Designer

    Tom Ford (April 2005): Launches the Tom Ford brand.

  • Model

    Naomi Campbell: Founds the charity Fashion for Relief.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: Appears in the Pirelli Calendar.

  • Model

    Doutzen Kroes: Voted Model of the Year by readers of Vogue.com.

  • Model

    Christy Turlington: Starts working with CARE on maternal health.

2006

  • Designer

    Tom Ford: Tom Ford Beauty launches with Estée Lauder, followed by the fragrance Black Orchid.

  • Designer

    John Galliano (Autumn/Winter 2006): A couture show in a Renaissance maze, with warrior women in gilded chain mail. Galliano takes his bow in a spacesuit.

  • Model

    Doutzen Kroes: Becomes a brand ambassador for L’Oréal Paris.

2007

  • Designer

    Miuccia Prada (Fall 2007): The “Fraggle Frocks” collection, with fraggly sweaters and decaying pieces.

  • Model

    Kate Moss: Launches a Topshop collection that reportedly sells out in a day, and a perfume line.

  • Model

    Doutzen Kroes (May 2007): Vogue cover as one of the “World’s Next Top Models”. Ranked 14th by Forbes.

  • Model

    Shalom Harlow: Ranked 13th among top-earning supermodels by Forbes.

2008

  • Runway

    Valentino: The Last Show — Spring/Summer 2008 · Paris.

  • Designer

    Valentino Garavani: He retires after a 45-year career, the year of the documentary Valentino: The Last Emperor. His last collection ended with every model in a red dress.

  • Model

    Doutzen Kroes: Ranked 5th by Forbes. Confirmed as a Victoria’s Secret Angel in August.

2009

  • Designer

    Tom Ford: His first film, A Single Man, earns Colin Firth an Oscar nomination for Best Actor.

  • Model

    Gisele Bündchen: Named Goodwill Ambassador of the UN Environment Programme.

  • Model

    Adriana Lima: Returns to the high-fashion runway for Givenchy.

  • Model

    Linda Evangelista: Included in the Met exhibition “The Model as Muse”.
