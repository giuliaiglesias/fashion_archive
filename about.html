ARCHIVE · The project

About

A virtual museum of fashion from 1990 to 2009.

What this is

ARCHIVE is a student project for a Webs & Apps Design course. It is a museum you can walk through online: the designers who shaped two decades of fashion, the runway shows people still talk about, and the models who gave them a face. It is meant for fashion students and anyone curious about the era.

What is inside

  • Designers: nine names, with their story and why they matter
  • Runways: nine shows, one per designer, each with a video
  • Models: nine faces connected to those shows
  • Timeline: 1990 to 2009, year by year
  • Mannequin Room: dress a mannequin with pieces from the shows
  • Quiz and Search

How it is built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no frameworks. The shared menu and footer are built by one small script. The Timeline, Search, Quiz and Mannequin Room are written in JavaScript and read their content from the same facts as the pages, so they cannot disagree with them. The site is hosted for free on GitHub Pages. The look is 90s magazine: black and grey paper, Bodoni Moda headlines, a typewriter font for text, and one red accent.

How AI was used

ARCHIVE is the author's idea and was art-directed by her. She built it together with an AI assistant, Claude, made by Anthropic, which she directed step by step. Here is who did what.

The author

  • Had the idea of a fashion museum and the goal behind it: a space that mixes entertainment and knowledge for fellow fashion lovers.
  • Chose the era (1990 to 2009), the designers, the runway shows and the models, from her own knowledge of fashion.
  • Set the art direction: the 90s magazine look, the colours, and the interactive features (timeline, Mannequin Room, quiz).
  • Compared several design options for most pages, rejected the ones that did not work (for example the first mannequins and the first model pictures), and chose the final versions.
  • Had the idea of Polaroids that recall backstage and castings, to solve the copyright problem with model photos. Said the Runways page was not original enough and asked for something more unique, then chose the front-row tickets from the options and pushed to make them look like real tickets.
  • Found and chose the runway videos, uploaded every version to GitHub, tested the live site and wrote the project memory in her own words.

The AI assistant

  • Wrote the HTML, CSS and JavaScript from the author's instructions.
  • Researched facts and wrote first drafts of the texts, which the author reviewed.
  • Drew the illustrations and ran automated tests (layout, links, keyboard, accessibility).

Checking the facts

Facts were checked against the pages on the Sources page. One mistake was found and corrected along the way.
