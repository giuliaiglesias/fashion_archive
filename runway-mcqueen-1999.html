McQueen: No. 13

Two robots, a white dress and a ballerina: fashion as performance.

Designer
Alexander McQueen
Name
“No. 13”, because it was his 13th show
Shown
September 1998, for the Spring/Summer 1999 season
Venue
Gatliff Warehouse, a former bus depot in Victoria, London
Length
More than 20 minutes
Opening
Aimee Mullins in hand-carved cherry-wood prosthetic legs designed by McQueen
Finale
Shalom Harlow and two robots from a car factory

What happened

The set was simple: bare floorboards and a lowered, lit ceiling. There were no celebrities in the front row, and McQueen even turned down a request from Victoria Beckham to attend. The clothes were mostly natural colors, tan, beige and ivory, made from raffia, cut and punched balsa wood, tiered lace and leather. The leather pieces included asymmetric belts, corsets and moulded harnesses.

McQueen was inspired by the workshops at Queen Mary’s Hospital in Roehampton that made artificial limbs for people injured in the First World War. The Paralympian Aimee Mullins opened the show wearing carved wooden legs he designed. In the first two set pieces, models spun on circular disks in the four corners of the stage.

The finale: the model Shalom Harlow, a former ballerina, danced on a fifth disk in the middle of the stage between two metal robots borrowed from a car factory. As she turned, the robots sprayed her pure white cotton dress black and yellow.

Why it mattered

It is often named as one of the most famous runway moments ever, because it showed that a fashion show could be a performance and an artwork, not only a way to sell clothes. McQueen later said he was inspired by High Moon, a 1991 installation by the artist Rebecca Horn.
