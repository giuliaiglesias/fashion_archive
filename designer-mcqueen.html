Who he is

Alexander McQueen learned tailoring as an apprentice on London’s Savile Row, and you can see it in his clothes: the cut is always sharp, even when the idea is wild.

In the 1990s he turned the runway into theatre. In 1995 he introduced the “bumster”, a very low-rise trouser. Later shows had models walking through water, standing in a ring of fire, and dressed as horned creatures. In 1996 he was named head designer at Givenchy in Paris while still running his own label.

Why it matters

His shows were about feelings, not only clothes: beauty and fear in the same room. The most famous moment is the end of “No. 13” in 1999, when two industrial robots sprayed paint onto a white dress while the model Shalom Harlow turned on a platform.

He shows that fashion can be art, and that a runway can be a story. He died in 2010 at the age of 40, and his work is still studied and exhibited.
