Who he is

Alexander McQueen learned tailoring as an apprentice on London’s Savile Row, and you can see it in his clothes: the cut is always sharp, even when the idea is wild.

In the 1990s he turned the runway into theatre. In 1995 he introduced the “bumster”, a very low-rise trouser. Later shows had models walking through water, standing in a ring of fire, and dressed as horned creatures. In 1996 he was named head designer at Givenchy in Paris while still running his own label.

Why it matters

His shows were about feelings, not only clothes: beauty and fear in the same room. The most famous moment is the end of “No. 13” (Spring/Summer 1999, shown in September 1998), when two industrial robots sprayed paint onto a white dress while the model Shalom Harlow turned on a platform.

He shows that fashion can be art, and that a runway can be a story. He died in 2010 at the age of 40, and his work is still studied and exhibited.

Moments to know

  1. Early onStarts work at sixteen on Savile Row, learns pattern cutting at the theatre costumers Bermans and Nathans, and works under Koji Tatsuno and later Romeo Gigli in Milan.
  2. 1995Introduces the “bumster” trousers and shows “Highland Rape”. His spring collection is named after Hitchcock’s The Birds.
  3. October 1996LVMH names him head of Givenchy, replacing John Galliano. He is also named British Designer of the Year.
  4. January 1997Presents his first haute couture collection for Givenchy.
  5. 1998Shows a collection in which a model stands in a ring of fire.
  6. September 1998“No. 13” (the Spring/Summer 1999 collection), held in the Gatliff Warehouse, a former bus depot in Victoria, London. Paralympian Aimee Mullins opens it in carved wooden legs designed by McQueen, and robots spray Shalom Harlow’s white dress black and yellow. McQueen said he was inspired by Rebecca Horn’s 1991 artwork High Moon.
  7. 2010He dies at the age of 40.
